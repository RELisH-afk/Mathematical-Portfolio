Theory

Differentiability occurs at a point “a” on a continuous part of f(x) when there is a single, well-defined tangent slope at that point. However, we must keep in mind that differentiability implies continuity, but continuity does not guarantee differentiability. This is where the difference quotient can aid us. The difference quotient shows the average rate of change (the slope of a secant line) between two points, “a” and “a + h.” As our step size h approaches zero, the point a + h approaches a, and the secant line approaches the tangent line, provided the derivative exists. Additionally, we must consider the left- and right-hand approximations of the difference quotient. If both approach the same finite value as h approaches zero, then the function is differentiable at that point. If they approach different values or fail to converge to a finite value, then the function is not differentiable at that point.

Help / Guide
